\section*{Summary}

\citet{wu2025bayesian} place a prior over binary selectors $z\in\{0,1\}^p$, where $z_j=1$ indexes a feature in the invariant set $S^*$ \citep{peters2016icp}.
The posterior $p(z\mid\mathcal{D})\propto p(z)\prod_{e,i}\hat{g}(Y^e_i\mid X^z_{ei})/\hat{p}_e(Y^e_i\mid X^z_{ei})$ rewards selectors for which the pooled conditional $\hat{g}$ dominates each environment-specific conditional $\hat{p}_e$.
Consistency (Thm~1) requires $\mu_{\min}>0$, the minimum KL divergence between $\hat{g}$ and any $\hat{p}_e$ over non-invariant features \citep[Def.~1]{wu2025bayesian}. Contraction (Thm~2) gives $\mathrm{TV}(p(z\mid\mathcal{D}),\,\delta_{z^*}) = O(2^p\,e^{-\kappa nE\mu_{\min}})$.
Both bounds assume squared loss under a linear model, and the product likelihood weights all environments symmetrically.
VI-BIP \citep[Sec.~4]{wu2025bayesian} scales this inference via mean-field variational approximation; its only empirical validation uses single-gene-deletion environments \citep[Sec.~5]{wu2025bayesian}, where hard interventions guarantee $\mu_{\min}>0$ by construction.

\section*{Critique}

\citet{peters2016icp}, \citet{wang2026negdro}, and \citet{wu2025bayesian} all enforce the same invariance condition under squared loss, yet what separates them is what the practitioner assumes about the environments before seeing data. BIP's symmetric product likelihood weights every environment equally, so its contraction rate $nE \gtrsim O(p/\mu_{\min})$ is bottlenecked by $\mu_{\min}$, the minimum KL divergence between pooled and local conditionals of $Y\mid X$. NegDRO measures heterogeneity differently: its identification requires $\lambda>0$, a spectral condition on the second moments of $X$ across environments \citep[Cond.~1b]{wang2026negdro}. Its tuning parameter $\gamma$ controls how negative the environment weights can go \citep[Eq.~9]{wang2026negdro}, with $\gamma=0$ recovering the symmetric simplex. A dataset can satisfy one heterogeneity condition but not the other, so the two frameworks are not merely different algorithms for the same problem.

Yet no theory characterizes where the boundary lies. There is no decision rule telling a practitioner when symmetric weighting wastes known structure, or when asymmetric weighting overfits to a misidentified dominant environment. The gap is acknowledged by neither paper.

The experiments do not close this gap. \citet[Sec.~5]{wu2025bayesian} validate VI-BIP exclusively on single-gene-deletion data where $\mu_{\min}>0$ holds by construction, the one regime where a practitioner \emph{could} justify asymmetric weighting and BIP's agnosticism is least needed. The empirical case for VI-BIP in observational or low-intervention settings, where symmetric weighting should matter most, remains unmade.

\section*{Questions}
Both questions below concern whether the practitioner-knowledge gap between BIP and NegDRO can be bridged.
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item NegDRO's rate improves smoothly in $\gamma$ \citep[Prop.~2]{wang2026negdro}, but this characterizes NegDRO alone. Is there a regime, characterized by the number of environments $E$, the ratio $\lambda/\mu_{\min}$, or the practitioner's uncertainty about which environment dominates, in which BIP's symmetric posterior provably outperforms NegDRO's asymmetric point estimate, or vice versa?
  \item Nonparametric extensions of ICP exist for the symmetric case \citep{heinzedeml2018nonparametric}, but no nonparametric analogue of NegDRO's asymmetric weighting has been proposed. Does the practitioner-knowledge distinction identified above survive when the loss is no longer quadratic, or does the symmetry-versus-asymmetry tradeoff collapse under nonparametric conditionals?
\end{enumerate}
