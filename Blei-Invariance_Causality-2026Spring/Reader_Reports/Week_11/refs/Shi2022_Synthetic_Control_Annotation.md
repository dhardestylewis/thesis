# Shi et al. (2022) — On the Assumptions of Synthetic Control Methods
## Full Annotation (line-by-line reading)

**Authors:** Claudia Shi, Dhanya Sridhar, Vishal Misra, David M. Blei
**Conference:** AISTATS 2022

---

## Title, Authors, Abstract
- **Problem:** Synthetic control methods estimate treatment effects using a weighted linear combination of untreated donor units. But *why* should a linear combination of outcomes perfectly capture unobserved complex confounding?
- **Contribution:** Formulates Synthetic Control geometrically by disaggregating "large units" (e.g. states, cities) into non-overlapping "small units" (individuals, parcels).
- **Key claims:** If the aggregate units are mixtures of the same underlying sub-populations, the linear SC weights mathematically correspond to matching the mixture proportions of the treated unit. Sub-population matching non-parametrically identifies the sub-population average treatment effect.

## Introduction and Motivation
- SC provides robust causal inference for panel data with a single treated unit.
- Standard justification relies on a linear factor model where weights perfectly cancel out time-varying latent confounders.
- **The Gap:** The linear factor assumption is strong and often opaque. Real-world policy data is rarely generated by clean linear combinations of latent variables.
- **Micro-foundations:** Most macro-units (states/cities) are aggregates of individual residents/entities. By shifting the level of analysis, linear SC naturally emerges from the aggregation of nonparametric sub-population effects.

## The Small Units Framework
- Data is defined by an observation matrix of aggregates `Y_{it}`, but fundamentally driven by small units `u`.
- Small units belong to unobserved sub-populations `k \in {1, \dots, K}`.
- Large units are discrete probability distributions over these sub-populations: `p(k|large unit)`.
- **Assumption: Invariant Sub-populations:** The potential outcomes for a small unit depends only on its sub-population `k` and the time period `t`, *not* on which large unit it belongs to.
- This creates an invariant causal structure: `Y_t(0) \perp Large Unit | Subpopulation`. This directly mirrors Invariant Causal Prediction structurally.

## Nonparametric Identification
- If the treated unit's sub-population distribution `p(k|Treated)` lies within the convex hull of the donor units' sub-population distributions, there exist non-negative weights `w_i` that perfectly reconstruct the treated unit's mixture.
- Once the sub-population mixture is reconstructed, the aggregate untreated potential outcome is exactly reconstructed across all time periods, without requiring any linear structural assumptions on how sub-populations respond to time.
- SC weights are simply sub-population matching weights.

## Discussion & Open Questions
- Identifiability requires that the treated unit does not contain a sub-population uniquely missing from the donor pool. If Austin has a specific flavor of tech-NIMBYism absent in all other Texas cities, SC fails.
- This framework provides a clear diagnostic: SC will fail if the support of the sub-populations drops out.
- Ties perfectly into Empirical Risk Minimization shifts and the Variance-Extrapolation penalties we evaluate for geographic domain shifts.

## Cross-paper Connections (Week 10 Thesis Context)
- **vs. ICP/V-REx:** ICP requires discovering stable conditionals $P(Y | \Phi(X))$ across all domains. This paper reveals SC is just doing ICP implicitly over the unobserved sub-populations $K$.
- **vs. Bayesian Optimization:** If SC relies on matching historical mixtures, then using BO to prescribe future policies must assume that the policy intervention does not shift the underlying sub-population assignments (i.e. gentrification displacing sub-population $K$). If it does, the SC donor baseline becomes structurally invalid.
