\section*{Summary}
\citet{wu2025bayesian} recast ICP as sparse Bayesian variable selection.
A binary selector $z \in \{0,1\}^p$ marks invariant features; the posterior
\(p(z \mid \mathcal{D}) \propto p(z)\prod_{e,i}\hat{g}(Y^e_i \mid X^z_{ei})/\hat{p}_e(Y^e_i \mid X^z_{ei})\)
up-weights selectors for which the pooled conditional $\hat{g}$ matches each local $\hat{p}_e$---a probabilistic analog of Peters et al.'s accept/reject invariance test.

Two theorems govern the posterior. \textbf{Consistency (Thm~1):} if $\mu_{\min}>0$ (positive KL between pooled and local conditionals on any non-invariant feature), the posterior concentrates on $z^*$. \textbf{Contraction (Thm~2):} mass on $\{z \neq z^*\}$ decays as $O(R\,e^{-\kappa nE\mu_{\min}})$, where $R=2^p$, $E$ is environments, and $n$ is per-environment $n$. Key implications: (i) data and environments trade off in the exponent---helpful given my $K\!\approx\!4$ regimes; (ii) $R=2^p$ is only a prefactor, so the dimensionality curse is milder than expected.

\textbf{VI-BIP} fits $q(z)=\prod_j \mathrm{Bern}(z_j;\pi_j)$ by ELBO maximization. The obstacle is differentiating through discrete $z$; the \textbf{U2G estimator} relaxes the discrete draw to a Gaussian surrogate, enabling standard stochastic gradient ascent. On a 6{,}170-gene perturbation dataset, VI-BIP matches exact BIP while being orders of magnitude faster.

\section*{Critique \& Contrast with the Optimization Approach}
\citet{fan2024negdro} replace the combinatorial posterior with a minimax problem
$\min_b \max_{w \in U(\gamma)}\sum_e w_e \hat{\mathbb{E}}[(Y^e-b^\top X^e)^2]$,
where $U(\gamma)$ permits negative weights. NegDRO yields a point estimate $\hat\beta^\gamma$ with polynomial-time convergence but no uncertainty quantification. The key difference: BIP exposes \emph{which} features remain ambiguous when environments are few; NegDRO gives a fast answer but not a sense of what additional data would resolve. For my $\sim\!4$-environment, $\sim\!50$-feature NIMBYism project, the multi-modal BIP posterior is directly informative about where more environment diversity is needed.

\section*{Questions}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item The contraction bound uses the population $\mu_{\min}$, not an estimate. Is there a pre-test analogous to classical ICP's global invariance check that certifies $\mu_{\min}>0$ before fitting BIP?
  \item VI-BIP's mean-field family ignores posterior correlations between $z_j$ and $z_k$. For strongly correlated regulatory features (zoning category, parking requirements), does mean-field bias $\pi_j$ toward 0.5? Would a structured variational family (e.g.\ Ising) recover sharper posteriors?
\end{enumerate}
