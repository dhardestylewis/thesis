# Project Reference Map: NIMBYism & Invariance

This document explicitly maps the course references to the "Invariant Predictors of NIMBYism" project, detailing the **Relationship** (theoretic link) and **Application** (practical step).

## 1. Core Framework: Invariant Risk Minimization
### **Arjovsky et al. (2019) - Invariant Risk Minimization**
*   **Relationship:** Provides the mathematical justification for why minimizing loss across environments leads to causal parents.
*   **Project Application:** This is the **Strategy**. We will define the loss function for our housing opposition classifier as:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{IRM} = \sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}_{train}} \text{CrossEntropy}(f(X_e), Y_e) + \lambda \| \nabla_{w|w=1} \mathcal{L}^e(w \cdot \Phi(X)) \|^2 $$
    Here, $\mathcal{E}_{train}$ are time blocks from 2007-2018 (Pre-Rewrite).

### **Peters et al. (2016) - Causal Inference using Invariant Prediction**
*   **Relationship:** Establishes "Invariance" as a proxy for "Causality". If a predictor (e.g., "Property Value") predicts Opposition with the same beta-coefficient in 2008 and 2024, it is likely a direct cause.
*   **Project Application:** We will use this for **Validation**. We will check if the features selected by our model have stable coefficients across our spatial environments (East vs. West Austin), unlike baseline models which likely flip signs.

## 2. Robustness & Stability
### **Bühlmann (2020) - Invariance, Causality and Robustness**
*   **Relationship:** Connects invariance to "Robustness" against distributional shifts.
*   **Project Application:** Justifies the **Importance** of the project. We frame the problem not just as "understanding NIMBYism" but as creating a "Robust Policy Tool" that won't fail when the market cycle turns (a distributional shift).

## 3. Representation Learning
### **Schölkopf et al. (2021) - Towards Causal Representation Learning**
*   **Relationship:** Discusses how to extract high-level causal variables from low-level inputs (text/images).
*   **Project Application:** We treat the **Protest Letters** (unstructured text) as observed variables $X$ generated by latent causal sentiments $S$ (e.g., "Fear of Traffic", "Change Aversion"). We aim to learn a representation $\phi(X)$ that recovers these invariant sentiments $S$, rather than correlating with specific keywords that might change over time (e.g., "ADU" vs "Granny Flat").

## 4. Empirical Bayes
### **Efron (2012) - Large-Scale Inference**
*   **Relationship:** Using the aggregate of many cases to inform the prior for a single case.
*   **Project Application:** We can treat each of the 10,000+ rezoning cases as a "parameter" to be estimated. We use the distribution of all cases to set an Empirical Bayes prior on the likelihood of opposition for a *new* case, shrinking our estimate towards the grand mean of that specific neighborhood type.
