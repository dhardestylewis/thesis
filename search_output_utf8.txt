40: This thesis asks whether the filing of a valid protest petition against a housing development proposal in Austin, Texas can be predicted before the project enters formal public hearings, using only information available at the time of filing. The primary outcome is a legally defined, directly observable event: whether adjacent property owners file a petition meeting the 20\% area threshold specified by Texas Local Government Code Chapter~211, thereby triggering a supermajority requirement at City Council. Working from an analytic sample of 7,074 discretionary zoning cases filed between 2007 and 2024, the core opposition model is evaluated under out-of-sample conditions: out-of-distribution bootstrap evaluation at the filing-date horizon yields a PR-AUC of \metricBootstrapFiling{} (95\% CI: \metricBootstrapFilingCI{}), with an Expected Calibration Error of \metricECE{} after Isotonic Regression---above the 0.05 threshold required for operational use. In-distribution 5-fold cross-validation provides an upper bound PR-AUC of \metricPRAUC{} with a top-decile lift of \metricTopDecileLift{} over the baseline petition rate. Filing-date administrative features contain meaningful signal for formal protest petition risk, but that signal is better suited to relative ranking than to forecasting that remains calibrated across policy periods. The false negative rate gap across council districts is \metricFNRGap{}, but given the sparsity of positive cases within individual subgroups, this figure likely reflects insufficient statistical power rather than genuine equalization of error rates. Supporting analyses include a regression discontinuity design at the 20\% petition threshold, which estimates a \metricRDDelay{}-day increase in council processing time (SE~$=$~\metricRDSE{}, 95\% CI: \metricRDCI{}), and a difference-in-differences estimator for the 2022 council electoral transition, which identifies a \metricFlipDiDCoeff{} shift in petition filing rates in districts that changed political representation ($p = \metricFlipDiDPval{}$). Semi-structured interviews with planning professionals contextualized these findings, highlighting the pre-2022 declining practical influence of the petition mechanism and the two distinct motivations driving public testimony. This thesis ultimately demonstrates that while administrative features contain strong predictive signal, the complex institutional realities of the zoning process are fundamentally difficult to capture through static model-based probabilities.
86: This literature review synthesizes scholarship across three core themes to directly motivate the thesis's methodological architecture. It first establishes the political economy of homeowner opposition and participatory distortion. Second, it isolates the specific institutional mechanism of the discretionary veto. Finally, it provides the intellectual justification for treating zoning conflict as primarily a predictive policy problem while acknowledging the inherent evaluative constraints required for algorithmic governance in spatial planning.
98: In Texas, this leverage is explicitly codified through the protest petition mechanism (Local Government Code \S~211.006). The statute legally empowers adjacent property owners to force supermajority city council voting requirements if owners representing 20\% of the \emph{area of lots} immediately adjoining the proposed change and extending 200 feet from that area sign a valid petition.\footnote{Note that \S~211.006(d) and (f) were repealed effective September 1, 2025 by HB 24 (89th Texas Legislature). The operative provisions now reside in new \S~211.0061, which establishes a three-tier protest system.} This mechanism formalizes the discretionary veto, transforming localized opposition into a formal institutional hurdle. Cellini, Ferreira, and Rothstein \cite{cellini2010schools}, in their analysis of California school bond referenda, demonstrate how supermajority thresholds inherently distort political outcomes to favor the status quo.
100: In Austin, the protest petition functions identically for land use: crossing the legal 20\% threshold triggers immediate voting friction and procedural delay. Recent empirical research by the Mercatus Center at George Mason University confirms the severity and asymmetry of this mechanism specifically within the Austin political geography. Their analysis demonstrates that valid petitions are more common for rezonings that increase residential density: historically, 25\% of rezonings to multifamily use in Austin faced a valid petition, compared to only 5\% for commercial rezonings.
123: Because the process is fundamentally asymmetric - empowering opponents of development through a fixed statutory buffer geometry - it creates a bias toward the status quo, dragging contested housing applications into months of procedural delays. The sheer scale of this localized gridlock ultimately forced intervention by the Texas legislature in 2025 via House Bill 24, which reformed the statute by stipulating that valid petitions against \emph{residential upzonings} are strictly ``expressive'' and no longer trigger the 3/4 supermajority vote requirement (though petitions against nonresidential rezonings remain fully intact). Consequently, predicting whether a qualifying protest petition will be filed against an application forms the core empirical challenge of this thesis.
133: While transitioning to a predictive architecture offers potential operational value for municipal planning, it introduces serious ethical risks. Barocas and Selbst \cite{barocas2016} demonstrate that predictive algorithms inevitably inherit the prejudices embedded in their training data. Because historical zoning data in American cities is inextricably linked to legacies of redlining, racial covenants, and exclusionary zoning \cite{whittemore2014racial}, the underlying spatial features, such as lot geometry, single-family zoning, and historic property values, function as proxies for race and class. If an algorithm learns that wealthy, historically white neighborhoods successfully file protest petitions, using that prediction to guide development decisions risks encoding a feedback loop that shields those neighborhoods from densification while concentrating development pressure on politically vulnerable communities \cite{richardson2019dirty}.

532: Pre-council attrition---withdrawal, stalling, or cancellation before a final council vote---is pervasive across the full analytic sample. Of the 7,074 cases filed, 6,163 (87.1\%) never reached a council reading. Among the \metricOpposedTotal{} cases that faced a valid protest petition, \metricOpposedWithdrawn{} (\metricAttritionRate{}) were withdrawn or stalled. Among the 6,663 unopposed cases, 5,844 (\metricUnopposedAttritionRate{}) dropped out. The opposed attrition rate is therefore \emph{lower} than the unopposed rate, suggesting that cases which attract formal petitions tend to be larger or more committed developments that persist further through the approval process, rather than being uniquely ``chilled'' by opposition. Attrition is the dominant mode of case resolution regardless of petition status.
534: Because attrition is so prevalent, predictive algorithms targeting terminal council votes suffer from severe survivorship bias: virtually all cases that reach a final vote are approved. The predictive architecture therefore models attrition itself---whether a case survives to council reading---rather than the near-deterministic terminal approval decision. By predicting pre-council dropout, Stage~D captures the institutional friction through which the approval process most often resolves.

689: \item \textbf{The Function of Opposition:} Suggesting that public opposition may also carry information rather than merely blocking projects, AURA board member Felicity Maxwell observed: ``We're not talking about suppressing opposition, but understanding it better to have more productive conversations.'' Prediction models formalize this understanding, summarizing patterns in observed opposition for earlier identification of contested cases.
690: \item \textbf{Asymmetric Transparency and Silent Supporters:} Jonathan Tomko (Principal Planner) emphasized that the municipal feedback infrastructure structurally lacks any avenue for \textit{anonymous} public testimony against a zoning case. Because neighborhood social dynamics often penalize residents who deviate from their neighborhood association's preservationist stance, Tomko noted that the formal opposition data is not strictly representative of the entire community. This aligns with Beaudet's observation that ``haters are overrepresented and silent supporters are underrepresented,'' which likely makes the observed opposition data an incomplete measure of community sentiment.
691: \item \textbf{Two distinct motivations and geography:} Marla Torrado (Displacement Prevention Division) stressed that opposition mechanisms capture completely distinct motivations. While the ``vocal opposition'' on the affluent West side leverage environmental overlays, East Austin utilizes opposition precisely to counter historic displacement. This context reinforces the severity of model miscalibration (ECE = \metricECE{}): using rigid probability thresholds could miss communities facing real displacement risk, failing ``to identify where people that are in actual need actually are'' because the algorithm cannot separate affluent NIMBYism from existential displacement defense.
694: These institutional realities underscore why the predictive tool performs best as a relative ranking mechanism rather than a high-confidence prediction tool.

853: \item \textbf{Classification Threshold:} To empirically ground the spatial error analysis and prevent misleading impressions caused by severe class imbalance, the binary prediction threshold for FNR evaluation is strictly anchored to the empirical background protest rate ($\mu_{y}$). Any localized zoning project with a predicted opposition probability exceeding the citywide historical baseline is classified as a predicted positive case, ensuring the algorithm's errors are evaluated against genuine physical prevalence rather than arbitrary percentiles.
