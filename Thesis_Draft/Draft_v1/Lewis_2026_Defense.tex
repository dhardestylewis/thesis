% ===== SLIDE 3: The Mechanism =====
\begin{frame}{The Protest Petition: An Institutional Veto}
    \begin{columns}
        \column{0.55\textwidth}
        \textbf{Texas LGC Chapter 211: The 20\% Rule}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Owners of 20\% of land within 200ft may file written protest.
            \item A valid petition triggers a supermajority ($\tfrac{3}{4}$) council vote.
            \item Post-2014 council (11 members): 6 votes $\rightarrow$ 9 required.
            \item 21 states maintain similar petition statutes.
        \end{itemize}
        \vspace{0.5em}
        \textit{A localized minority can block citywide housing decisions.}

        \column{0.45\textwidth}
        \centering
        \vspace{0.5em}
        \resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{%

% ===== SLIDE 7: Rarity Problem =====
\begin{frame}{The Rarity Problem}
    \begin{columns}
        \column{0.5\textwidth}
        \textbf{Sample Characteristics}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item $N = \metricBaselineCases{}$ cases, 2007--2024.
            \item Base rate: $\approx\metricBaseRate{}$ (1 in 38 cases).
            \item Test-set positives: $n_{\text{pos}} = \metricNTestPositive{}$.
        \end{itemize}
        \vspace{0.8em}
        With so few events, confidence intervals are wide. The model output is a \textbf{score per case} (0--1); cases are ranked by that score, not classified by threshold.

        \column{0.5\textwidth}
        \textbf{Evaluation Design}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Train on years $\leq t$; evaluate on years $> t$.
            \item Temporal holdout for the test set; random cross-validation within training.
        \end{itemize}
        \vspace{0.8em}
        \textbf{Primary metric: PR-AUC}

        \small AUROC omitted: we are predicting a rare class.
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}
\note{\textbf{[2 min]} PR-AUC over AUROC is the correct metric for rare-class prediction. The temporal holdout is critical: a random split leaks future mobilization patterns into training. Fifteen test-set positives explains the wide CI ([0.48, 0.91]).}

% ===== SLIDE 12: What Drives Petition Risk =====
\begin{frame}{What Drives Petition Risk}
    \begin{columns}
        \column{0.4\textwidth}
        \textbf{Key Finding}
        \vspace{0.5em}

        Tree ensembles weight the \textbf{physical ask}: parcel scale (44\%) and zoning density request (36\%) together account for 80\%+ of attribution.

        \vspace{0.8em}
        Deep models also weight parcel scale (55\%) but add \textbf{neighborhood social fabric}: demographics (18\%) and housing tenure (12\%).

        \vspace{0.8em}
        Same data, same outcome; different internal logic.

        \column{0.6\textwidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[height=0.68\textheight, width=\linewidth, keepaspectratio]{exhibits/fig_feature_importance_clustered_H0_Full.pdf}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}
\note{\textbf{[2 min]} Lead with the figure. Tree ensembles: parcel scale (44\%) + zoning density request (36\%) = 80\%+ of attribution. The model is detecting how big the project is and how much density change is being asked for. Deep models keep parcel scale dominant but add demographics (18\%) and housing tenure (12\%) -- they weight who lives nearby alongside the physical ask. This sets up the next slide: these may be two different mechanisms of NIMBYism captured by different model architectures.}

% ===== SLIDE 15: Signal Dilution =====
\begin{frame}{Signal Dilution: When Retraining Hurts}
    \begin{columns}
        \column{0.45\textwidth}
        \textbf{A Counter-Intuitive Finding}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Pre-2018 models achieve $\sim$34$\times$ lift in 2024.
            \item Retraining on modern data degrades lift to $\sim$19$\times$.
        \end{itemize}
        \vspace{0.8em}
        Post-ordinance labels starve the model of positive examples. The frozen legacy model preserves spatial sorting intelligence from an active institutional era.

        \column{0.55\textwidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth, height=0.65\textheight, keepaspectratio]{exhibits/fig_temporal_drift_lift_testyear.pdf}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}
\note{\textbf{[2 min]} Retraining actively hurts: exposing the model to the post-ordinance era stops it from penalizing aggressive spatial configurations because the institutional environment has stopped producing petitions. Implication: continuous retraining would lose predictive power as the policy environment stabilizes.}

            \item Rigid cutoffs could miss communities facing real displacement risk. (Torrado) \textrightarrow{} supports triage ranking over hard thresholds.
            \item Formal opposition data lacks anonymous channels; it is not representative of the full community. (Tomko) \textrightarrow{} the training labels are incomplete by design.
        \end{itemize}

% ===== SLIDE 17: Limitations =====
\begin{frame}{Limitations}
    \begin{columns}
        \column{0.5\textwidth}
        \textbf{Methodological}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \textbf{Chilling effects}: Only filed cases observed; pre-emptive withdrawals are invisible. Selection correction was not feasible.
            \item \textbf{Timestamp imputation}: Milestone dates approximated from statutory minimums.
            \item \textbf{Unobserved certification}: Outcome is reconstructed area share, not the clerk's legal determination.
        \end{itemize}

        \column{0.5\textwidth}
        \textbf{Ethical and Governance}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \textbf{Proxy discrimination}: Neighborhood income and demographics encode class and race dynamics even without protected-class inputs.
            \item \textbf{Motive blindness}: Cannot separate exclusionary NIMBYism from anti-displacement resistance.
            \item \textbf{External validity}: Parameters are Austin-specific and pre-HB~24 only.
        \end{itemize}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}
\note{\textbf{[2 min]} Address proactively. The chilling-effect limitation is the most substantive: predicting on a censored subset of the decision space. Proxy discrimination cannot be fully resolved by excluding protected-class features. The study maps these as boundaries, not solved problems.}

% ===== SLIDE 18: Conclusion =====
\begin{frame}{Conclusion}
    \small
    \textbf{What we showed}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Crossing 20\% adds \metricRDDelay{} days of processing time: the outcome is worth predicting.
        \item Filing-date data ranks petition risk above chance (PR-AUC = \metricBootstrapFiling{}, CI: \metricBootstrapFilingCI{}), with top-decile lift of \metricTopDecileLift{}.
        \item Algorithm choice determines which feature clusters drive predictions, a normative decision.
    \end{itemize}

    \textbf{What surprised us}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Retraining on modern data degrades lift; legacy models preserve spatial sorting intelligence.
        \item Performance ceilings are consistent with mobilization as a strategic social choice.
    \end{itemize}

    \textbf{What it implies}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Predictions belong in relative triage rankings, not case-level probability thresholds.
        \item Methodology is portable; Austin-specific parameters are not.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
\note{\textbf{[2 min]} Connect back to the opening arc: institutional costs established first (RD), then prediction capability characterized, then limits mapped. Real signal, real limits. Lead with the RD result; it anchors the "so what."}

% ===== SLIDE 19: Policy Implications =====
\begin{frame}{Implications: A Now-Closed Policy Window}
    \textbf{The HB 24 Shift (September 1, 2025)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Texas HB 24 exempted residential upzonings from the supermajority requirement.
        \item The 20\% institutional veto no longer applies to the most contested case types.
        \item The institutional regime is closed; its demographic and spatial correlates of opposition remain visible in the predictions.
    \end{itemize}

    \vspace{1.2em}
    \textbf{What travels forward}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Methodology (time-stamped features, temporal holdouts, semantic clustering, qualitative integration) is portable to other discretionary review systems.
        \item New York, Iowa, Oklahoma, and Arizona retain active petition statutes. The framework applies wherever institutional veto mechanisms create predictable opposition patterns.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
\note{\textbf{[1.5 min]} Not a live policy problem; a historical analysis of how a specific mechanism operated. The analytical contribution is the methodology. Iowa (20\% at 200ft), Oklahoma (tiered), and Arizona (20\% land plus 20\% lots) retain comparable statutes. The framework is designed to travel.}

\textbf{Bauer}: Qualitative sample size? $N=8$, purposive, not statistically representative. Used for grounding, not hypothesis testing.

\textbf{Begley}: Could this be used against communities? Yes, and the ethics warning is deliberate. Governance safeguards, not technical fixes, are the appropriate response.

\textbf{General}: What extends this? HB 24 natural experiment, pre-hearing public comment text, other city replication.}
