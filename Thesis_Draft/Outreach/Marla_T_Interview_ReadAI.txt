Research Interview
The meeting reviewed a study combining Austin protest-letter records with machine learning and qualitative interviews to predict and interpret neighborhood opposition to development. Daniel Lewis described assembling protest letters back to 2007 alongside property and tax records (coverage through about 2018–2019) to train predictive models and to inform a qualitative phase assessing community views; he said the project will be fully open source with a complete model report. The models emphasize census-tract demographics and name-derived owner/neighborhood differences as predictive features.

Participants discussed model inputs, calibration, and ethical risks, including how arbitrary thresholds could misclassify areas or people and how a public predictive tool might enable developer targeting. Daniel and Marla debated likely stakeholders and uses, with local government outreach and developer decision-making framed as primary applications but noted divergent incentives. Marla T outlined the City of Austin’s Displacement Prevention Division (established 2019), its three teams, and displacement pressures concentrated in historically Black and brown eastern neighborhoods. She summarized Project Connect’s anti-displacement funding structure and the Community Advisory Committee’s role in prioritizing funds into development, preservation/acquisition, and community-initiated solutions, offered to share a spending dashboard, and cautioned that quantitative tools should be paired with qualitative safeguards to protect households not served by the market.
