### Version B (More Specific)
> Subject: Thesis Reader Request: Predicting Zoning Opposition (Daniel Hardesty Lewis)
>
> Hi Professor [Last Name],
>
> I am a Columbia M.S. Urban Planning student finishing a thesis that models and predicts zoning opposition (NIMBYism) at the parcel level using public records (zoning cases, protest petitions/public testimony where available, parcel ownership/value attributes, and spatial proximity features). I am reaching out to ask if you would consider serving as a thesis reader.
>
> My goal is to produce a high-impact, defensible empirical workflow that (1) predicts opposition likelihood for a given case and parcel, (2) distinguishes business vs. individual owners, (3) supports interpretable drivers of opposition, and (4) explicitly audits leakage and temporal validity so results generalize beyond one city and time period.
>
> I would value your guidance on:
> * How to frame the contribution relative to existing housing supply / land-use politics literature
> * What validity checks would be most convincing (across cities, cycles, and policy regimes)
> * What outputs would be most useful to planning agencies and researchers
>
> The commitment is lightweight: one brief meeting to align on scope and one read of the draft near submission. If helpful, I can send a 1-page project memo and a link to the current outline.
>
> Would you be open to this?
>
> Best regards,
> Daniel Hardesty Lewis
> Columbia University
