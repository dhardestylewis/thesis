40: This thesis asks whether the filing of a valid protest petition against a housing development proposal in Austin, Texas can be predicted before the project enters formal public hearings, using only information available at the time of filing. The primary outcome is a legally defined, directly observable event: whether adjacent property owners file a petition meeting the 20\% area threshold specified by Texas Local Government Code Chapter~211, thereby triggering a supermajority requirement at City Council. Working from an analytic sample of 7,074 discretionary zoning cases filed between 2007 and 2024, the core opposition model is evaluated under out-of-sample conditions: out-of-distribution bootstrap evaluation at the filing-date horizon yields a PR-AUC of \metricBootstrapFiling{} (95\% CI: \metricBootstrapFilingCI{}), with an Expected Calibration Error of \metricECE{} after Isotonic Regression---above the 0.05 threshold required for practical application. In-distribution 5-fold cross-validation provides an upper bound PR-AUC of \metricPRAUC{} with a top-decile lift of \metricTopDecileLift{} over the baseline petition rate. Filing-date administrative features contain meaningful signal for formal protest petition risk, but that signal is better suited to relative ranking than to forecasting that remains calibrated across policy periods. The false negative rate gap across council districts is \metricFNRGap{}, but given the sparsity of positive cases within individual subgroups, this figure likely reflects insufficient statistical power rather than genuine equalization of error rates. Supporting analyses include a regression discontinuity design at the 20\% petition threshold, which estimates a \metricRDDelay{}-day increase in council processing time (SE~$=$~\metricRDSE{}, 95\% CI: \metricRDCI{}), and a difference-in-differences estimator for the 2022 council electoral transition, which identifies a \metricFlipDiDCoeff{} shift in petition filing rates in districts that changed political representation ($p = \metricFlipDiDPval{}$). Semi-structured interviews with planning professionals contextualized these findings, highlighting the pre-2022 declining practical influence of the petition mechanism and the two distinct motivations driving public testimony. This thesis ultimately demonstrates that while administrative features contain strong predictive signal, the complex institutional realities of the zoning process are fundamentally difficult to capture through static model-based probabilities.

428: Because the baseline opposition frequency is highly skewed, raw predicted probabilities from gradient boosting models suffer from persistent overconfidence. To address this, the architecture applies out-of-fold Isotonic Regression, which fits a non-decreasing, non-parametric monotonic function mapping predicted probabilities to empirical outcome frequencies, enforcing calibration within the evaluation loop. Alongside standard Expected Calibration Error (ECE), the evaluation also reports the Brier Score and Adaptive Calibration Error (ACE = \metricACE{}). At a classification threshold of $P \geq 0.50$, the model achieves precision of \metricPrecisionAtFifty{} and recall of \metricRecallAtFifty{}, flagging \metricNFlaggedAtFifty{} of 7,074 cases. The Brier score is \metricBrierScore{}.
472: Under simulated bootstrap evaluation at the filing-date horizon, the opposition model achieves a PR-AUC of \metricBootstrapFiling{} (95\% CI: \metricBootstrapFilingCI{}). To contextualize this magnitude within the broader universe of spatial social science---where state-of-the-art stochastic rare-event models projecting conflict or civic unrest often achieve PR-AUC values in the 0.30--0.45 range due to fundamental spatial sparsity \cite{hegre2019views}---this \metricBootstrapFiling{} metric represents meaningful discrimination relative to the low base rate. The model simultaneously exhibits an Expected Calibration Error (ECE = \metricECE{}) above the 0.05 calibration reliability threshold. Under in-distribution 5-fold cross-validation, which provides an optimistic upper bound, the PR-AUC is \metricPRAUC{} with a top-decile lift of \metricTopDecileLift{} over baseline. Given the sample base rate of \metricBaseRate{} (\metricBaseRateN{} cases), this lift corresponds to an exact top-decile precision of \metricTopDecilePrecision{} (\metricTopDecileTP{} true positives among \metricTopDecileN{} cases)---substantial separation, but far from deterministic. At a classification threshold of $P \geq 0.50$, the model achieves precision of \metricPrecisionAtFifty{} and recall of \metricRecallAtFifty{}, flagging \metricNFlaggedAtFifty{} of 7,074 cases. The Brier score is \metricBrierScore{}.
476: After Isotonic Regression calibration, the model reports an Expected Calibration Error (ECE) of \metricECE{} (95\% bootstrap CI: \metricECEBootCI{}) and a calibration slope of \metricCalibrationSlope{}. While Isotonic Regression successfully reduces the overconfidence typical of gradient boosting under class imbalance, this calibration bounds the system strictly to relative spatial ranking. This expected error restricts deployment within automated decision-making pipelines, signaling that raw probabilities cannot be treated as deterministic.

532: Pre-council attrition---withdrawal, stalling, or cancellation before a final council vote---is pervasive across the full analytic sample. Of the 7,074 cases filed, 6,163 (87.1\%) never reached a council reading. Among the \metricOpposedTotal{} cases that faced a valid protest petition, \metricOpposedWithdrawn{} (\metricAttritionRate{}) were withdrawn or stalled. Among the 6,663 unopposed cases, 5,844 (\metricUnopposedAttritionRate{}) dropped out. The opposed attrition rate is therefore \emph{lower} than the unopposed rate, suggesting that cases which attract formal petitions tend to be larger or more committed developments that persist further through the approval process, rather than being uniquely ``chilled'' by opposition. Attrition is the dominant mode of case resolution regardless of petition status.
534: Because attrition is so prevalent, predictive algorithms targeting terminal council votes suffer from severe survivorship bias: virtually all cases that reach a final vote are approved. The predictive architecture therefore models attrition itself---whether a case survives to council reading---rather than the near-deterministic terminal approval decision. By predicting pre-council dropout, Stage~D captures the institutional friction through which the approval process most often resolves.
617: The Difference-in-Differences (DiD) event-study estimate for council dissent, operationalized as the total number of ``no'' votes recorded per zoning case at the final council reading, is $\metricDiDVotes{}$ (SE $= \metricDiDSE{}$, 95\% CI: $\metricDiDCI{}$). This estimate is non-informative: the coefficient, standard error, and confidence interval are all identically zero, indicating insufficient variation in the outcome within the estimation window to identify a treatment effect. The specification uses a Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE) model with case-level and time fixed effects, excluding post-treatment covariates to avoid ``bad control'' bias \cite{cinelli2020making}. Given the short post-treatment window available for HOME Phase~1, these results should not be interpreted substantively.
